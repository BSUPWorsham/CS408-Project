SSDI, SSI, and benefits for disabled children

Social Security runs two disability programs with different rules. Many people qualify for one, some for both. Here is how each works and how a claim moves from application to appeal.

SSDI

Social Security Disability Insurance

For people who worked and paid Social Security taxes long enough to earn "work credits." Your monthly payment is based on your past earnings, and Medicare coverage begins after a waiting period.

Why age 50 matters: Social Security's rules recognize that it is harder to switch to a new kind of job later in life. If you are 50 or older and can no longer do your past physical work, you may qualify even if you could still do some lighter work.

SSI

Supplemental Security Income

A needs-based program for adults and children with disabilities who have limited income and resources. Work history is not required. In most states, SSI also opens the door to Medicaid.

Some people receive SSI and SSDI at the same time when their SSDI payment is low.

Children & adult children

SSI for children and Childhood Disability Benefits

Children under 18 can receive SSI when a condition causes very serious limits in daily functioning and the household meets income limits.

Adult children whose disability began before age 22 may receive benefits on a parent's record once the parent is retired, disabled, or has passed away.

The stages of a claim

Each stage has a deadline, usually 60 days from when you receive the decision letter. Missing it can mean starting over and losing months of back pay.

We can take your case at any stage, but the earlier we start, the stronger the record we can build.
